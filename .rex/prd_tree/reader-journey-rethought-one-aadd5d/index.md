description: "A holistic review of the site as built (Week 36 edition, 24 pages, rendered at 390 and 1280 px) and of the two pending site features (the ask-a-question input and contributor credit). The pieces are individually good and collectively over-served: the same eleven models and three questions are presented on too many surfaces, the next feature was about to add a third navy call-to-action block to a home page that already has two, and the forkability the whole project is built on stops at the copy.\n\n**What the review found, in order of consequence.**\n\n1. *The report page is six rosters long.* /reports/hot-dog/ is 11,780 px tall on desktop and 27,711 px on a 390 px phone, about seventy phone screens. It lists the same eleven models in the framing matrix, the verbatim list, the edition-wide bars, the quadrant, the leaderboard, eleven scorecards, eleven vendor profiles, and the data table. On a phone the responsive work stacks the leaderboard, the scorecards, the profiles and the table into four consecutive runs of eleven labeled cards each. Reflow passed; the page reads as tolerated, which is what the responsive epic said it must not.\n\n2. *Charts with no variance.* Every model scores decisiveness 1.00 this edition, so the quadrant plots eleven points along its top edge with their numbers overlapping, every radar scorecard is the same pentagon, and the leaderboard carries a column of identical 1.00s. The constructed scores are fine as a joke; a chart that cannot separate anything is not a chart.\n\n3. *The headline word is clipped on phones.* On the answer board at 375 and 390 px, a row that carries the REASONING tag shows its answer as \"N…\" or \"Y…\": `.word` has `white-space: nowrap` and `text-overflow: ellipsis`. The responsive gate checks overflow, not truncation, so it passed; the project's own `.responsive/home@375.png` shows the defect. This is the one word the site exists to show.\n\n4. *Four indexes of three things.* The home hand-off block, /reports/, /history/ and /runs/ each list the same three questions from the same one edition. History is in the primary nav and says \"trends start with the second edition.\" The question switcher is implemented four ways (hero tabs, alignment-grid headers, history pills, rack cards) and a report page has no way to reach the other reports except the nav. Stale copy from the last restructure: the edition archive page says \"current reports are on the home page,\" and a report's empty state says \"see the archive\" but links /history/.\n\n5. *Framing-arm pages are duplicate reports.* /reports/hot-dog/asserted/ re-renders the quadrant, leaderboard, scorecards, profiles and table under a system prompt. The composite score is verdict-agnostic, so the standings are nearly identical to the control's; the page admits the control is canonical. Nine full report pages where three reports and a comparison would do.\n\n6. *The fork is still branded.* The fork build test proves no sandwich text leaks; it does not check the brand. A fork's site says HOTDOG BENCHMARK in the masthead, title, feeds and llms.txt, \"Prepared by Hotdog Benchmark, an En Dash research program\" on every report, and its fork block tells visitors to clone en-dash-consulting/hotdogbenchmark. `REPO_URL` and `SITE_NAME` are constants. The pending ask-a-question item says its URLs derive from \"the site's own name,\" and there is no site name to derive from.\n\n7. *The ask-a-question design sends the visitor away.* Enter in the field is a cross-origin GET to endash.us that opens a consulting site's contact modal, with the email address requested as free text and no visible outcome afterward. The GitHub route works for every fork; the contact route works for one deployment. The item's own note mentions \"canon and gallery cadences,\" a product decision (contributed questions on a cheaper cadence than the founding three) that appears nowhere else in the PRD and that determines what a submitter is promised.\n\n8. *Small defects a reader sees.* The history page reads \"Week 36, 2026 was run 4 times, on September 2, 2026, September 2, 2026, September 2, 2026, September 2, 2026.\" \"Thinking alike\" names three pairs \"and 22 more\": with three binary questions, 25 of 55 pairs agree, so the line says the statistic is not selective. The phone nav is a scrolling pill row with \"HOW IT WORKS\" and \"ABOUT\" cut at the edge and no scroll affordance. The feeds exist only as `<link rel=\"alternate\">`; a weekly publication shows no next-edition date and no way to subscribe. The manual screen-reader pass is marked blocked by the CI-gates item, which is complete.\n\n**The approach.** Fewer surfaces, each doing one job. The report shows every model once, with the arms as a comparison rather than as three reports. One question switcher component everywhere a question can change. Contribution becomes a pipeline with a visible state (proposed, scheduled, live) in the registry, so a submitted question appears on the site with credit when its pull request merges, before it costs a cent; the GitHub route is primary because it forks, and the En Dash contact route is a configured option. A `site.json` carries the name, the byline, the repository and the contact route, so a fork is a fork. And two gate gaps close: a truncation check, and a plot-variance check that drops a chart axis with nothing on it.\n\nThe children carry the specifics and an order: the phone defects and gate gaps first (they are small and visible today), then the report diet, then the switcher and index consolidation, then the site config, then the question pipeline, and the ask-a-question input last, on top of the pipeline it needs. The two existing pending items in the Fork-this epic (the ask-a-question input and contributor credit) are revised in place to build on this."
